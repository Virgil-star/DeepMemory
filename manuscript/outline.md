# HEIR ÆTERNA

## Chapter-by-chapter beat sheet

Forty-four chapters and a coda, in three acts. Close third on Iris throughout,
past tense, no other viewpoint — the reader learns what she learns, when she
learns it. Act I covers one autumn when she is sixteen. Act II opens on a jump to
nineteen and runs four years. Act III is eleven days.

Each entry carries the **turn** (what is now true that was not true before) or the
**cost** (what it took). Chapters with neither are not yet chapters.

---

## ACT I — THE INHERITANCE

*A girl born on a day that does not exist.* Chapters 1–14. One autumn, age sixteen.

**1. The Missing Day**
She steals a day-slip and a note out of her father's atlas. The six-day world
laid down as lived texture, not exposition. At dinner she asks whether there was
ever a seventh day and he answers truthfully without answering at all. Curtain:
an envelope in the post slot, addressed in her own handwriting.
*Cost: the first theft. She is now someone who keeps things from him.*

**2. Forty-Three Years**
She reads the letter. Dated forty-three years out. Two claims: her father is not
the first inventor of time travel, and she must never let him correct the mistake
he calls her existence. She compares the hand to her own, letter by letter, and
it matches in the places a forger would not know to match.
*Turn: she has a secret with a future version of herself.*

**3. Six Days, Recited**
School. The continuity drill. A boy uses the wrong word and is walked kindly to
the nurse; nobody mentions it afterward. Bo Teague, who makes her laugh. The two
sentences she has been taught all her life — *the calendar has always contained
six days* and *there have been no Tuesdays for nineteen years* — stand next to
each other in her head for the first time.
*Cost: she learns that noticing is classified as a symptom.*

**4. A Citizen May Request Her Own File**
The Registry. Her birth record prints a day-name field that should be impossible
to populate: TUESDAY. The clerk goes very still, and prints it anyway, because
the system printed it. Iris copies the record out by hand before she leaves.
*Turn: documentary proof, in her own handwriting.*

**5. Clerical Error**
She puts it in front of her father. He says the words — clerical error — and he
also says the one true thing, *you were not an error*, and then he takes the
certificate and does not give it back. She still has her copy.
*Cost: she learns he will remove evidence from her hands while meaning every
word of his love.*

**6. The Custodian's Day**
His work, properly seen for the first time: the Foundry, the numbered findings,
a custodian reading other people's histories and officially deciding nothing. She
sees a ledger of displaced hours without yet being able to read it.
*Seed: one debt line carries the year of her birth.*

**7. Audit**
Auditor Sabine Clerke at the door. Household consumption shows a day nobody
logged. Edmund handles her beautifully. Iris lies for the first time, lies well,
and is believed.
*Turn: Clerke marks the house and does not stop thinking about it.*

**8. The Gray Countries**
The atlas again. The gray boundaries are not dead nations; they are unobtaining
ones. On the flyleaf, in the hand from the binding: *Thea Moravec, Fourth
Concordance.*
*Turn: her mother has a provenance rather than a grave.*

**9. Pell**
The shuttered laundry. Pell reads the slip's serial, tells her it is cut rather
than issued, and tells her exactly whose hours they were: a woman two streets
away who sold a month to pay for a funeral. Pell will not take it back, and is
not sorry, and is not lying.
*Cost: her stolen Tuesday has a victim with an address.*

**10. The Visiting Room**
She spends the Tuesday. Inside the unlogged hour she meets a man of the Fourth
Concordance who knew her mother and will not say whether Thea is alive. He says:
*she had a condition, and she filed it.*
*Turn: a right of refusal exists on paper somewhere.*

**11. Ansel**
First full dream. A house she has never lived in, a staircase, a brother who
remembers her and is not frightening. The rule is set for the reader in the same
breath: impressions, never facts.

**12. The Woman in the Tenement**
She goes to the address. The woman does not remember selling the month and has
missed her daughter's wedding without knowing why. Iris cannot give it back. She
pays what she can, which is nothing that helps.
*Cost: she has done, with her own hands and at small scale, exactly what her
father does.*

**13. The Condition**
Office of Claims. Grandin, procedural and not unkind, confirms a sealed
instrument lodged in her name: unreadable until she is of age or files to open
it, and opening it notifies the other party.
*Turn: her mother left her a weapon with a tripwire on it.*

**14. What He Wanted Her For** — *act break*
She puts it all in front of him: Moravec, the Fourth Concordance, the sealed
instrument. He tells her the truth, all of it, with love, as a reason. *I made
you so they could not delete us. Your mother agreed.*
*Act turn: her parents have been arguing about the purpose of her life since
before she existed.*

## ACT II — THE CHILDREN OF IMPOSSIBLE HISTORIES

*The truth about her conception.* Chapters 15–31. Age nineteen to twenty-three.

**15. Nineteen**
Jump. The new equilibrium: she has not opened the instrument, he has not stopped
arranging her life, and they love each other across a table neither will clear.

**16. The Shape of an Arrangement**
The pattern surfaces. Every friend who frightened him moved away. Every teacher
who encouraged her questions was reassigned. Each instance is benign. The
aggregate is not.
*Turn: he has been engineering her consent for eleven years.*

**17. Clerke's Ledger**
She goes to the Auditor instead of running from her. A trade: an orphan serial
for a lesson in how to read a debt line.

**18. Her Birth Year on the Ledger**
Chapter 6 pays off. The Sixth Settlement — the Tuesdays, taken from everyone —
serviced a debt incurred by the First Concordance three years before she was
born, by her father's first intervention.
*Turn: the missing day of her birth and the missing day of her world are the
same transaction.*

**19. The Market, Properly**
Scale. Who buys, who sells, which districts are harvested. The poor keep the
calendar running, and the arithmetic is not hidden, merely unexamined.

**20. The Fourth Concordance**
A bought week gets her to what is left of her mother's history: the unobtaining,
living inside purchased hours. They know her face before she speaks.

**21. What Her Mother Was**
Not a victim he rescued. An engineer of the Fourth Concordance's own foundry, who
lost. She came to Reality Zero to contest, not to be saved.
*Turn: the marriage was an alliance between opponents.*

**22. Her Father's Record**
The findings, read by people they were filed against: Edmund Rask's custodianship
has repeatedly preserved the First Concordance at the direct cost of other
populations. Not malice. Triage, performed for decades, in private, by one man.

**23. A Designation, Not a Provenance**
The central reveal. The First Concordance is not demonstrably the original
reality — it is the history the Reconciler adjudicated first. Reality Zero is a
filing order. Her father mistook an assignment for a moral entitlement.

**24. Mireille**
She meets Mireille Dax, claimant of the Ninth Concordance: a woman with a
daughter, a kitchen, and a causal claim stronger than Iris's entire world.

**25. Ansel, Specific**
The dream yields a staircase that matches a house in the Fourth Concordance.
Inadmissible, as always. She goes anyway, and finds the document that is not.

**26. The Sealed Instrument**
She files to open her mother's condition. The tripwire fires. Edmund is notified
within the hour and says nothing about it for four days.

**27. The Right of Refusal**
Her mother's hand. An independently enforceable right to refuse to be used — and
a second clause he never mentioned: Thea's own recorded testimony contesting the
Fourth Settlement, held in trust for whoever would one day stand it up.
*Turn: Iris is not only a claimant. She is an executor.*

**28. The Engineering**
Confrontation. He does not deny the arrangements; he explains them as care, and
he is not entirely wrong, which is the problem. She asks it: *who gave you the
right to decide which reality deserves to exist?*

**29. His Answer**
He has one, and it is good. Nobody gave him the right. Somebody had to hold it.
The ones who were deleted were not asked either, and he is the only person alive
who has ever had to choose with the numbers in front of him. The novel lets him
land this punch undiminished.

**30. Thea**
The mother resolves. She is not alive. She spent herself contesting the Fourth
Settlement and the contest failed; what survives is the testimony in trust.
*Cost: Iris has spent four years taking instruction from a dead woman, and must
now decide something her mother never specified.*

**31. The Ninth** — *act break*
A finding issues. The Ninth Concordance's claim measures stronger; the First is
scheduled for settlement. To preserve it the administration requires Iris's
certification — which means certifying Mireille's world out of existence.
*Act turn: the instrument her father built her to be is finally needed, and the
price has a face and a kitchen.*

## ACT III — THE RIGHT TO EXIST

*An appeal against eternity.* Chapters 32–44. Eleven days.

**32. Certification**
The Office of Claims. The form. Grandin explaining, kindly and exactly, where to
sign.

**33. Refusal**
She refuses. Not a speech. A blank line on a form.

**34. The Consequence of a Blank Line**
Deletion proceeds. The first losses are small, local, and irreversible: a street,
a school, Bo Teague's family. The novel bills her for her own integrity before it
lets her be right.

**35. The Wider Filing**
Rather than certify, she files the continuity injunction against the
reconciliation process itself — on the ground that the Reconciler's mandate rests
on consent that was inherited, and therefore void.

**36. Void**
The argument, stated properly. The founders accepted the terms on behalf of the
unborn. Postulate IV holds that consent cannot be inherited. The Reconciler's own
validity check therefore condemns its mandate. Clerke's ledger is the evidence.
Thea's testimony in trust is the precedent.

**37. Her Father's Side**
He is called to testify against her, because preserving his world requires it. He
does. And he undercuts his own testimony in a way only she is close enough to
catch, and will never admit to afterward.

**38. What She Cannot Save**
The honest accounting, named rather than abstract: not the Fourth Concordance,
not the Tuesdays, not the woman in the tenement's month, not Bo's family. Enumerated
out loud, in a room, with nobody comforted.

**39. The Proof**
In the Foundry's provenance archive she reads the anonymous proof that made the
machine possible, and recognises the hand. It is hers. She has not written it yet.

**40. Three Doors**
She works out all three options and their prices — submit it unchanged, submit it
altered, refuse entirely — and nobody tells her which to take, because nobody
can.

**41. Pell's Price**
To stand in the Foundry during a settlement she needs hours nobody has allocated.
Pell will sell them and, as always, says exactly whose. She buys. The novel pays
it on the page and does not look away.

**42. The Altered Proof**
She writes it. The injunction goes in as a mathematical necessity rather than an
administrative courtesy: no administration can suspend it, because the machine
cannot exist without it. The price is eleven years of the Foundry's early
interventions.

**43. Settlement**
The structure reconciles and the losses arrive as billed. The early interventions
never happened. With them goes her father's custodianship, and with that his
memory of her.

**44. Distributed Custodianship**
The new arrangement, and not a victory: unilateral revision suspended, surviving
evidence preserved, custody distributed among claimants, and a docket of erased
histories now entitled to contest — which is years of work for other people.
Mireille's world obtains. So does Iris's, weaker, and with its Tuesdays still
gone.

---

## CODA — The Day You Were Born

Edmund Rask, no longer a custodian: a man with a good mind and a thin file, in a
small flat, with an atlas. Iris visits. He does not know her. He is courteous, and
curious about her work, and offers her tea.

On his table the oversized atlas lies open, the boundaries of unobtaining
countries drawn in gray, and pressed into the binding, in a hand he cannot place,
a note that reads *This was the day you were born.*

She leaves it where it is.

She does not forgive him, and she does not condemn him, and she does not complete
the future he imagined for her. She finishes her tea, and goes back to work.

## Structural notes

**The three-act spine as a single question.** Act I: *what am I?* Act II: *what
was I made for?* Act III: *what am I going to do about it?* If a chapter does not
advance one of those three, it is decoration.

**Rationing the mechanism.** The reader should not meet the words *continuity
injunction* until chapter 13, *dual provenance* until chapter 21, and
*distributed custodianship* until chapter 44. Iris learns the vocabulary of the
system at the same rate she learns she is a part of it.

**The father's curve.** Chapters 1–14 he is a loving man with evasions.
Chapters 15–31 he is a loving man with a programme. Chapters 32–44 he is a loving
man on the other side of the table. He never becomes a villain, and he never
stops being right about the thing he is right about.

**The ledger as recurring instrument.** Introduced unreadable (ch. 6), taught
(ch. 17), decoded against her own birth (ch. 18), and entered as evidence
(ch. 36). Four appearances, each one closer to her.

**Where the novel must not flinch.** Chapter 12 (she harms someone exactly as he
does), chapter 29 (his answer is good), chapter 34 (her integrity has victims),
chapter 38 (the enumerated unsaved), chapter 41 (she buys hours cut out of a
named stranger). Cut any of the five and the book becomes the easy version of
itself.
