# HEIR ÆTERNA · BOOK ONE

# THE INHERITANCE

*What am I?* — Thirty-eight chapters. One autumn and the winter after. Iris is
sixteen.

**Mode: episodic.** A sixteen-year-old making discrete incursions into a world.
Each chapter is a raid, loosely strung. This is permission granted once and never
again in the trilogy — Book Two is driven from its first page.

**She becomes:** an heir.
**It costs her:** the house. Not her father; the trust. In chapter 31 she betrays
him, knowingly, to buy her own immunity.

---

## PART ONE — The Anomaly (1–12)

*She discovers that she is impossible.*

**1. The Missing Day**
She steals a day-slip and a note out of her father's atlas. The six-day world laid
down as lived texture, not exposition. At dinner she asks whether there was ever a
seventh day and he answers truthfully without answering at all. Curtain: an
envelope in the post slot, addressed in her own handwriting.
*Cost: the first theft. She is now someone who keeps things from him.*

**2. Forty-Three Years**
She reads the letter. Two claims: her father is not the first inventor of time
travel, and she must never let him correct the mistake he calls her existence. She
compares the hand to her own, letter by letter, and it matches where a forger
would not know to match.
*Turn: she has a secret with a future version of herself.*

**3. Six Days, Recited**
School. The continuity drill. A boy uses the wrong word and is walked kindly to
the nurse; nobody mentions it afterward. Bo Teague, who makes her laugh. The two
sentences she has been taught all her life — *the calendar has always contained
six days* and *there have been no Tuesdays for nineteen years* — stand next to
each other in her head for the first time.
*Cost: she learns that noticing is classified as a symptom.*

**4. A Citizen May Request Her Own File**
The Registry. Her birth record prints a day-name field that should be impossible
to populate: TUESDAY. The clerk goes very still, and prints it anyway, because the
system printed it. Iris copies the record out by hand before she leaves.
*Turn: documentary proof, in her own handwriting.*

**5. Clerical Error**
She puts it in front of her father. He says the words. He also says the one true
thing — *you were not an error* — and then takes the certificate and does not give
it back. She still has her copy.
*Cost: he will remove evidence from her hands while meaning every word of his
love.*

**6. The Custodian's Day**
His work, properly seen: the Foundry, the numbered findings, a custodian reading
other people's histories and officially deciding nothing. She sees a ledger of
displaced hours without being able to read it.
*Seed: one debt line carries the year of her birth.*

**7. Audit**
Auditor Sabine Clerke at the door. Household consumption shows a day nobody
logged. Edmund handles her beautifully. Iris lies for the first time, lies well,
and is believed.
*Turn: Clerke marks the house and does not stop thinking about it.*

**8. The Gray Countries**
The atlas again. The gray boundaries are not dead nations; they are unobtaining
ones. On the flyleaf, in the hand from the binding: *Thea Moravec, Fourth
Concordance.*
*Turn: her mother has a provenance rather than a grave.*

**9. Pell**
The shuttered laundry. Pell reads the slip's serial, tells her it is cut rather
than issued, and tells her exactly whose hours they were: a woman two streets away
who sold a month to pay for a funeral. Pell will not take it back, is not sorry,
and is not lying.
*Cost: her stolen Tuesday has a victim with an address.*

**10. The Visiting Room**
She spends the Tuesday. Inside the unlogged hour she meets a man of the Fourth
Concordance who knew her mother and will not say whether Thea is alive. He says:
*she had a condition, and she filed it.*
*Turn: a right of refusal exists on paper somewhere.*

**11. Ansel**
First full dream. A house she has never lived in, a staircase, a brother who
remembers her and is not frightening. The rule set for the reader in the same
breath: impressions, never facts.

**12. The Woman in the Tenement** — *part turn*
She goes to the address. The woman does not remember selling the month and has
missed her daughter's wedding without knowing why. Iris cannot give it back. She
pays what she can, which is nothing that helps.
*Cost: she has done, with her own hands and at small scale, exactly what her
father does.*

## PART TWO — The Investigation (13–27)

*She works the system, and the system works back.*

**13. The Condition**
Office of Claims. Hollis Grandin, procedural and not unkind, confirms a sealed
instrument lodged in her name: unreadable until she is of age or files to open it,
and opening it notifies the other party.
*Turn: her mother left her a weapon with a tripwire on it.*

**14. Bo**
A chapter of ordinary life, placed here deliberately. Bo Teague, his mother's
kitchen, a six-day week lived by people who have never once questioned it. The
reader must love this to feel Book Three. Nothing investigative happens.
*Function: the world worth saving, established before anyone threatens it.*

**15. The Nurse's Office**
She deliberately triggers a continuity referral to see the machinery from inside.
A kind room, a kind woman, a form. In the waiting area, a patient who remembers
Tuesdays and has been treated for nineteen years.
*Cost: she now has a file.*

**16. The Price of an Hour**
She wants another slip and cannot afford one. What she has to trade is information
about her own household. She does not trade it yet.

**17. What Clerke Wants**
Clerke approaches her directly rather than the house, over tea, pleasantly. An
offer: tell me where the slip came from and the file goes away. Iris refuses, and
Clerke seems pleased.

**18. The Flyleaf**
Tracing Thea Moravec through the Registry. The Fourth Concordance as a record
rather than a rumour — a numbered Settlement with a sealed content list.

**19. Her Father's Study**
She searches it properly. Finds his correspondence with the Office of Claims, in
his own hand.

**20. The Agreement**
He agreed in writing to an independently enforceable right of refusal. Signed,
witnessed, lodged beyond his reach. So why has he never mentioned it in sixteen
years?
*Turn: his silence is the tell, not his words.*

**21. Ansel's Staircase**
Second dream, more specific. She draws the staircase on the back of her copied
birth record because it is the paper to hand — an image the trilogy will use
again.

**22. The Man Who Knew Thea**
Her second bought hour. Thea was an engineer, not a refugee. She came to Reality
Zero with credentials.

**23. An Alliance Between Opponents**
She understands the marriage was a contest. Her whole childhood recontextualises
in an afternoon: the arguments she half-remembers were negotiations.

**24. The Debt Line**
Clerke teaches her to read a ledger line, in exchange for nothing, which is
frightening. She does not tell Iris what this particular line says.

**25. Her Birth Year**
She decodes it herself. The Sixth Settlement — the Tuesdays, taken from everyone —
serviced a debt incurred by the First Concordance three years before she was born,
by her father's first intervention.
*Turn: the missing day of her birth and the missing day of her world are the same
transaction.*

**26. Confrontation, Attempted**
She tries to put it to him and cannot. She loses her nerve at the table, talks
about school instead, and hates herself for it.
*Function: a failure beat. She is sixteen, and the book must let her fail.*

**27. The Tripwire** — *part turn*
She files to open the instrument.

## PART THREE — The Truth, and What It Costs (28–38)

**28. Four Days of Silence**
He has been notified. He says nothing. The house becomes a held breath. He cooks,
he asks about her day, he is perfect.

**29. Grandin, Again**
The instrument can be opened only with the second party present. She will have to
sit beside her father while a stranger reads her mother's conditions aloud.

**30. Audit, Second Visit**
Clerke arrives with the orphan serial traced to Iris. Leverage, at last, and
Clerke has been waiting for exactly this moment. A choice: give up Pell, or be
charged.

**31. What She Gives Up**
She gives up neither. She gives Clerke the ledger line — the one that implicates
her father's first intervention in the taking of the Tuesdays — in exchange for
her own immunity. She knows what she is doing while she does it.
*Cost: she betrays him before he betrays her. The trilogy sits on this chapter.*

**32. The Tenement, Again**
The woman's daughter's wedding has happened without her. Iris attends it as a
stranger, uninvited, standing at the back. She tries to make it right and cannot.

**33. The Opening**
The instrument is opened. A registrar reads Thea Moravec's hand aloud in a small
room with Edmund Rask sitting three feet away.

**34. The Right of Refusal**
The clause: an independently enforceable right to refuse to be used. And the
second clause — Thea's own recorded testimony contesting the Fourth Settlement,
held in trust for whoever would one day stand it up.
*Turn: he did not know about the second clause. Iris watches him learn it. His
face is the chapter.*

**35. What He Wanted Her For**
He tells her everything, in the car, calmly, with love, as a reason. *I made you
so they could not delete us.*

**36. Her Mother Agreed**
And: *your mother agreed.* It is true. It is the worst sentence in the book and
she has to carry it.
*Cost: she cannot even have the clean grievance of having been made by one parent
against the other.*

**37. The Letter, Re-read**
She re-reads the forty-three-year letter with everything she now knows, and it
reads completely differently. *The mistake he calls her existence* — he has never
once called her a mistake. Not in sixteen years. Not tonight, when he had every
reason to.
*Turn: someone else called her that. The letter is not about her father.*

**38. Nineteen Is Three Years Away**
She does not run and does not forgive. She chooses to stay in the house, and to
learn, and to wait — and the choosing is cold and deliberate and nothing like a
child's. Last line forward-leaning, not resolved.

## Where Book One stands at the close

**Answered completely:** what she is, who made her, why, and that her mother
consented to her existence and refused her use.

**Open, by design:** what she intends to do. That is a decision declined, not an
answer withheld.

**Outstanding debts the trilogy must pay:** the woman in the tenement's month
(Book Three, ch. 38); the orphan serial (Book Three, final docket); Bo Teague
(Book Three, ch. 6 and the docket); the ledger line she traded (Book Two — Clerke
uses it, and Iris has to live with having supplied it); the staircase drawing
(Book Two, ch. 25).

**Withheld absolutely:** the designation reveal, the programme, Thea's death, the
other children, and every mention of the proof.
