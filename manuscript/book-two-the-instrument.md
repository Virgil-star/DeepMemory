# HEIR ÆTERNA · BOOK TWO

# THE INSTRUMENT

*What was I made for?* — Thirty-six chapters. Four years, intermittent. Iris is
nineteen to twenty-three.

**Mode: driven.** Not episodic, from the first page. A single escalating line of
pursuit. The world needs no introducing and gets none.

**She becomes:** an executor.
**It costs her:** the mother. Not a death scene — the end of the possibility of
rescue.

---

## PART ONE — The Programme (1–12)

**1. A Sequential Serial**
No summary of the missing three years. She buys a day-slip over a counter, issued
stock, sequential serial, receipt in hand, and signs the register with her own
name. The girl who stole a cut slip out of an atlas now transacts inside the
system. One object does the work of a chapter of exposition.

**2. The Shape of an Arrangement**
Every friend who ever frightened him moved away. Every teacher who encouraged her
questions was reassigned. Each instance benign.

**3. The Aggregate**
She builds the list — nineteen names, eleven years, one pattern. Undeniable on
paper and deniable in every single conversation.
*Turn: he has been engineering her consent since she was eight.*

**4. What Clerke Did With It**
The ledger line Iris traded in Book One has had consequences. Her father was
investigated and cleared. He never learned who supplied it, and he talks about
his acquittal at dinner with relief, and she has to sit there.
*Cost: Book One's betrayal comes back not as exposure but as his gratitude.*

**5. The Market, Properly**
Scale. Who buys, who sells, which districts are harvested. The arithmetic is not
hidden, merely unexamined: the poor keep the calendar running.

**6. Others**
The title promise. There are other children of impossible histories. Dual
provenance is not unique to her; it is a technique, and other custodians have
used it.
*Turn: she is not a miracle. She is a model.*

**7. The Ones Who Signed**
Most of them certified when asked. They are comfortable, protected, well
pensioned and hollow, and one of them is kind to her in a way she cannot bear.

**8. The One Who Refused**
One refused, eleven years ago, and was not protected — her provenance was
successfully challenged as fabricated and the injunction collapsed. Iris finds her
working in a laundry two districts from Pell's.

**9. Fabrication**
The mechanism of that challenge, learned properly. Standing can be voided by
disproving an origin. Her father's insurance has a hole in it and he has known
about the hole for twenty years.

**10. Pell's Districts**
Pell, older, still honest, still naming whose hours he sells. He will not sell her
a crossing. Someone else will.

**11. Ansel, Specific**
The dream yields the staircase again, and this time she has the drawing she made
at sixteen on the back of her copied birth record. Inadmissible, as always, and it
tells her where to go.

**12. Crossing** — *part turn*
A bought week gets her into what is left of her mother's history.

---

## PART TWO — The Mother's World, the Father's Record (13–26)

**13. The Unobtaining**
Living inside purchased hours: a community that exists only while someone is
paying. They know her face before she speaks.

**14. What Her Mother Was**
Not a victim he rescued. An engineer of the Fourth Concordance's own foundry, with
credentials and a reputation and a side.

**15. The Foundry That Lost**
Two machines, one Settlement. The Fourth built theirs eight years after Reality
Zero built his, which is the whole of why they lost.

**16. Her Father's Record**
The numbered findings, read aloud to her by the people they were filed against.
His custodianship has repeatedly preserved the First Concordance at the direct
cost of other populations.

**17. Triage**
The arithmetic laid out, defensible line by line and monstrous in total. Nobody in
the room thinks he enjoyed it. That is not the complaint.

**18. A Designation, Not a Provenance**
The central reveal. The First Concordance is not demonstrably the original
reality. It is the history the Reconciler adjudicated first.

**19. Filing Order**
What that means, worked through until it is unbearable: her father's entitlement
is clerical. Reality Zero is a position in a queue. He has spent thirty years
defending a baseline because it was *numbered* one.

**20. Mireille**
Mireille Dax of the Ninth Concordance. A kitchen, a daughter, a stew that needs
another twenty minutes.

**21. A Stronger Claim**
The Ninth's numbers. Iris checks them herself, twice, and cannot argue with them.

**22. Thea's Trail**
Pursuing her mother through the unobtaining, who are evasive in the particular way
of people protecting someone from a disappointment.

**23. Thea**
She is dead. She spent herself contesting the Fourth Settlement and the contest
failed. There is no hiding place, no reunion, no instruction beyond what she
already left.
*Cost: the end of rescue. Iris has spent seven years taking direction from a dead
woman.*

**24. The Testimony in Trust**
She reads it in full for the first time: her mother's argument, complete,
footnoted, and still unfiled because Thea ran out of standing before she ran out
of case.
*Turn: it is the argument Iris will eventually file. It has been waiting.*

**25. Executor**
She accepts the role. Quietly, alone, with nobody to witness it, which is the
point.

**26. Home** — *part turn*
She returns to Reality Zero with everything, and says nothing for eleven days.

---

## PART THREE — Confronted, and Required (27–36)

**27. Eleven Years**
She puts the list of nineteen names on the table.

**28. The Engineering**
He does not deny it. He explains it as care, and he is not entirely wrong, which
is the problem — to him the arrangements and the love are one act and always were.

**29. Who Gave You the Right**
She asks it. The question the whole trilogy is built to arrive at.

**30. His Answer**
Nobody gave him the right. Somebody had to hold it. The deleted were not consulted
either, and he is the only person alive who has ever had to choose with the
numbers in front of him. The novel lets him land this undiminished and does not
answer him in the chapter.

**31. The Thing He Will Not Say**
There is one more thing and he will not say it. She names the withholding and he
admits it is a withholding and still will not say it.
*Function: the proof, seeded and never explained. This is as far as Book Two goes.*

**32. Clerke, Across the Table**
Clerke is an ally now, and it has cost her a career she was good at. She mentions
this once, lightly, and never again.

**33. The File That Called Her a Mistake**
Book One's letter pays off. In a Ministry file from the year before her birth,
somebody wrote the word. Not her father. The administration had a view about her
before she existed.

**34. Fabrication, Filed**
Her provenance is challenged. Standing suspended pending review.

**35. Nobody**
Nine days as a person with no recognised claim in any history. She cannot file,
cannot testify, cannot be heard. Her father is not composed, for the first time in
her life, and it frightens her more than the suspension does.

**36. The Demand**
The finding issues: the Ninth Concordance's claim measures stronger and the First
is scheduled for settlement. To preserve it the administration requires Iris
Rask's certification — which she cannot give, because it has just ruled she may
not exist.

---

## Where Book Two stands at the close

**Answered completely:** what she was made for, including the part her father never
intended. He made her an obstacle; her mother made her an executor.

**The instability:** she ends the volume legally nonexistent and formally
indispensable at the same time. Less stable than it began, which is the whole of
the rule.

**Withheld absolutely:** the proof, its authorship, and the three doors.
